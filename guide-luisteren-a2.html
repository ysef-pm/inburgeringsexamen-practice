Inburgeringsexamen luisteren (A2): what to expect and how to practise

Updated September 2026

For a lot of people, luisteren is the scariest part: people speak fast, words blur together, and there's no scrolling back. The good news is that A2 listening questions are about practical information, and catching that is a trainable skill.

The format at a glance

Exam partLuisteren, level A2 (Wet inburgering 2013 route)
HowOn a computer with headphones; audio fragments and short films, then questions
Time45 minutes
Cost€50 per attempt

What trips people up

  • Numbers and times. Half negen is 8:30, not 9:30. Kwart over drie, tien voor half vijf — drill these until they're instant.
  • Negations. "De bus rijdt vandaag niet" — one small word, opposite answer.
  • Changed plans. Speakers often say one thing and then correct it: "Dinsdag… nee, woensdag kan ik beter." The question is about the final version.
  • Losing focus after one missed word. If you miss a word, let it go and keep listening — the answer often comes later.

How to practise luisteren

  1. Read the question before the audio. In practice and in the exam. You'll know whether to listen for a time, a place or a reason.
  2. Daily short listening. 10–15 minutes of everyday Dutch beats an hour once a week. NOS Journaal in Makkelijke Taal and slow Dutch podcasts are good starting points.
  3. Drill clock times and numbers separately — they're worth a surprising number of points.
  4. Practise with exam-style questions, not just passive listening. Answering under time pressure is the actual skill.
