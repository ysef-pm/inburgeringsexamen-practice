Inburgeringsexamen lezen (A2): format and a strategy that works

Updated September 2026

Lezen is the part most people pass comfortably — if they manage their time and don't try to understand every word. It's a test of finding information in everyday texts, not of vocabulary depth.

The format at a glance

Exam partLezen, level A2 (Wet inburgering 2013 route)
HowOn a computer at a DUO exam location, multiple choice
Time65 minutes
Cost€50 per attempt

DUO doesn't publish a fixed number of questions or a raw pass mark for the A2 reading exam, so treat any number you see online as approximate.

What kind of texts you'll read

  • Letters and emails — from the gemeente, a school, a landlord, an employer
  • Notices and signs — opening hours, rules in a building, a changed schedule
  • Adverts and announcements — a course, a job, something for sale
  • Short information texts — about a service, an event, a health tip

A strategy that saves points

  1. Read the question first. Then you know what you're looking for — a date, a price, a reason, an action.
  2. Scan for the spot. Look for the word from the question (or a synonym) in the text. Read that part carefully; skim the rest.
  3. Watch the small words. niet, geen, alleen, pas, al, nog flip meanings — "je hoeft geen formulier mee te nemen" is the opposite of what a fast reader catches.
  4. Eliminate. Usually one option is clearly wrong and one is a trap that repeats words from the text but says something else. Pick the one that matches the meaning.
  5. Don't get stuck. 65 minutes is enough if you keep moving. Flag and come back.

Words that show up again and again

A2 texts reuse the same practical vocabulary. If these are automatic, you'll read much faster: aanmelden, afspraak, afzeggen, betalen, gratis, verplicht, meenemen, ophalen, openingstijden, gesloten, vanaf, uiterlijk, per maand, de huur, de gemeente, het formulier, de handtekening.
