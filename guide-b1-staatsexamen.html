The B1 inburgering exam (Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I): what to expect

Updated September 2026

If you arrived in the Netherlands after 2021, you've probably heard that the inburgering bar went up from A2 to B1. That's true for most people on the new route — and the B1 exams are a real step up in length and difficulty. Here's what you're actually facing.

A2 or B1: which applies to you?

Integration obligation started…LawLanguage level
before 1 January 2022Wet inburgering 2013A2
on or after 1 January 2022Wet inburgering 2021B1 (standard route)

Your personal requirements — including your route and deadline — are in Mijn Inburgering (log in with DigiD). On the A2 route? See the A2 writing guide instead.

The four B1 language exams

On the Wi2021 B1 route, the language exams are the Staatsexamen NT2 Programma I:

PartFormatTime
LezenSix texts in a paper booklet; multiple-choice answers on the computer110 min
LuisterenAround 40 multiple-choice tasks on audio and short films; each fragment plays once90 min
SchrijvenTyped on a computer: 8 sentence tasks, 2 partial-writing tasks, 2 short writing tasks100 min
SprekenComputer with headset: 8 short and 8 medium-length spoken tasks~25–30 min

Each part costs €50 and is scored separately: you need at least 500 scaled points on every part. A great reading score can't rescue a failed speaking exam. KNM (€50, 45 minutes) is a separate DUO exam — see the KNM guide.

What makes B1 harder than A2

  • Length and stamina. Lezen alone is almost two hours. Practise full-length sets, not just single texts.
  • Opinions and reasons. At B1 you're expected to give your view and back it up — ik vind dat…, omdat…, aan de andere kant… — in both speaking and writing.
  • Register. Writing tasks switch between formal (a complaint to a company) and informal (a message to a colleague). Getting the tone right is part of the score.
  • Listening once. No replay — you need to catch the key information the first time.

How to prepare

  1. Take a level check first to see whether you're close to B1 or still building from A2.
  2. Practise writing and speaking with feedback — they're the parts you can't score yourself, and the ones where B1 differs most from A2.
  3. Do full timed rounds of lezen and luisteren in the final weeks.
  4. Book early. Exam places can be scarce; check the rooster on staatsexamensnt2.nl.
